## Minimum actual consumer experiment

Once the source-derived difficult case and its expectations are accepted, implement only that bounded case in Rust, presenting the same pending choices through a small Bevy tabletop and a test driver. The renderer consumes committed game consequences; animation completion must not decide rules. A local explicit implementation is sufficient; no DSL, generalized card interpreter or runtime LLM is justified.

The human task should complete a meaningful interaction: start the selected skill test, choose whether and how Jacqueline intervenes, resolve every supported branch to its consequence, inspect why it happened, save at a nested pending choice, reopen, finish, and narrate it using replay Next/Back. Use original selected-card identities and a source-justified starting state. Deterministically constructed state is a test facility and does not prove complete scenario setup or ordinary campaign progression.

Small meshes for cards, tokens and standees plus an English card/choice panel are the least complex presentation candidate. One absent image and an entirely absent archive must both preserve these decisions. Compare control burden with Joel's existing physical/digital routine; its superiority is currently unmeasured. This experiment can reject Bevy or the proposed execution approach, but cannot establish full campaign fidelity, repeated enjoyment, or a reusable engine.

## Continuity and agent contracts to challenge

- **Save:** preserve input revisions, supported application version, deck/zone contents, pending decisions and continuations, temporary modifiers, per-round limits, random state and already revealed outcomes. Autosave after committed decisions; a manual save at a pending nested choice must load without revealing extra information, rerolling or applying effects twice. An unreadable or incompatible save fails visibly while preserving the original. Atomic replacement and a retained previous save are a proportionate first recovery approach.
- **Replay:** retain committed decisions, random outcomes and consequences with their governing source/code identities. Next/Back operates on presentation moments without changing the match. Compare the resumed and uninterrupted outcomes, then inspect the same sequence through the actual replay UI; a seed alone is insufficient. Camera motion is presentation state. Keep original records and an available compatible build initially; do not promise automatic migration to every future build.
- **Agent:** expose the same player-visible observations and legal pending choices used by the human UI, and submit choices to the same Rust execution function. Outdated or illegal choices must be refused. Keep hidden decks, chosen draws and direct state edits in a separately privileged fixture interface. Automated fidelity checks require the independent source expectations; merely finishing an agent run is not evidence of correctness or enjoyable use.

These are proposed minimum consequences of the play contract, not a storage schema. For the isolated case, an initial engineering estimate is **8–16 active agent hours** spanning rule implementation, tabletop controls and continuity checks, plus **two human sessions of at most 20 minutes** for interaction and narration. These are uncertain planning estimates, not an allowance or campaign estimate. The main assessment owns the proposed appetite and next decision. Stop if the governing case cannot be settled, actual UI access is unavailable, or the agreed allowance ends; preserve the narrower result rather than substitute a headless demonstration.
